Food data sources

Last updated 4 October 2026

When you search for a food in Fikko, the calories and nutrients come from the public databases below. We thank the organisations that maintain them.

Food composition values are averages. The nutrient content of a food varies with the season, how it was grown and prepared, the brand and the portion, so treat every figure as a close estimate rather than an exact measurement.

Australian Food Composition Database (AFCD)

Food composition data for Australian foods comes from the Australian Food Composition Database, © Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ), published under a Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 Australia licence (licence terms; see also FSANZ’s Data User Licence Agreement).

Changes we made: we use only each food’s energy, protein, carbohydrate and fat. We convert energy from kilojoules to calories and show values per 100 g. We have not otherwise altered the data. Fikko is not endorsed by FSANZ.

Limitations: there are limitations associated with food composition databases. The values may represent an average of the nutrient content of a sample of foods and ingredients measured at one time, and can vary substantially between batches and brands because of changes in season, processing practices, ingredient source and methods of calculation.

Australian data: this database is based on Australian foods, and Australian data may not suit other countries.

USDA FoodData Central

Whole foods and prepared dishes come from FoodData Central, published by the U.S. Department of Agriculture, Agricultural Research Service. It is in the public domain.

Open Food Facts

Packaged products and barcode lookups use Open Food Facts, a collaborative database whose contents are available under the Open Database Licence. Contains information from Open Food Facts.

Your own foods and estimates

Foods you add yourself use the values you enter. When the AI estimates the calories in a meal from your voice or a photo, Fikko marks it as an estimate.
